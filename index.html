Understand your estimate

How SahodPH calculates your estimated take-home pay

Your gross salary is not always the same amount you receive in your bank account. In the Philippines, employees may have mandatory government contributions and withholding tax deducted from their pay. SahodPH brings these items together to give you an estimated monthly take-home pay.

1. Start with your gross salary

Gross salary is the amount you earn before deductions. Depending on your compensation, this may include your basic monthly salary, taxable allowances, overtime pay, night differential, bonuses, or other taxable earnings.

If you only want a basic estimate, enter your monthly basic salary. Use Advanced mode when you have additional earnings that you want to include in the calculation.

2. Estimate your SSS contribution

SSS is one of the government contributions that may be deducted from an employee's salary. The employee share depends on the applicable contribution schedule and salary credit used for the calculation. SahodPH shows the estimated employee contribution as part of your salary breakdown.

3. Estimate your PhilHealth contribution

PhilHealth contributions are also considered when estimating your monthly take-home pay. The calculation uses the applicable premium rate and the income limits used by the calculator, with the estimated employee share shown as a monthly deduction.

4. Estimate your Pag-IBIG contribution

Pag-IBIG is another mandatory employee contribution included in the estimate. The amount depends on the applicable compensation tier and contribution limit. SahodPH displays the estimated employee share in your salary breakdown.

5. Determine taxable compensation

Not every amount you receive is necessarily treated the same way for tax purposes. SahodPH separates taxable and non-taxable inputs in Advanced mode so you can see how they affect the estimate.

Employee contributions are also considered when estimating the withholding tax on taxable compensation.

6. Estimate withholding tax

Withholding tax is an estimated income-tax deduction based on the applicable Philippine monthly tax brackets. Your actual employer payroll calculation may differ because payroll can include additional compensation details, exemptions, adjustments, or other applicable rules.

7. Calculate your estimated take-home pay

After estimating the applicable government contributions and withholding tax, SahodPH subtracts these deductions from your gross monthly income.

Basic calculation:
Gross salary − government contributions − withholding tax = estimated take-home pay

Why your take-home pay is lower than your gross salary

When you see a job offer such as ₱30,000 per month, that amount is generally discussed as gross salary. The amount you actually receive can be lower after applicable deductions.

Your payslip may contain government contributions, withholding tax, and other deductions that are not included in this calculator. SahodPH focuses on the standard deductions modeled by the calculator so you can get a quick estimate before reviewing your actual payroll.

What SahodPH does not include

Your actual take-home pay may be different from the estimate shown here. Employers can have additional payroll deductions or adjustments that are specific to your employment.

  • Company loans or salary advances
  • Insurance or HMO deductions
  • Employer-specific benefits and deductions
  • Absences or undertime
  • Other payroll adjustments not entered into the calculator

Common questions

Philippine salary calculator FAQs

What is the difference between gross salary and take-home pay?

Gross salary is your earnings before applicable deductions. Take-home pay is the amount left after the deductions included in the calculation. Your actual payslip may also contain employer-specific deductions that are not modeled by SahodPH.

What salary deductions does SahodPH calculate?

The calculator estimates employee SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG, and withholding tax. It also shows the combined deductions and estimated take-home pay.

Can I include overtime and allowances?

Yes. Use Advanced mode to enter applicable taxable allowances, non-taxable allowances, overtime pay, night differential, and bonuses or incentives.

Is the result exactly the same as my payslip?

Not necessarily. SahodPH provides an estimate. Your employer's payroll may include additional deductions, benefits, adjustments, exemptions, or compensation rules that are not included in the calculator.

Can I use this calculator to compare job offers?

Yes. Comparing estimated take-home pay can help you understand the difference between two gross salary offers. You should also consider benefits, bonuses, allowances, work schedule, and other parts of the compensation package.

Why is my actual salary different from the calculator?

Actual payroll can differ because of company-specific deductions, benefits, absences, undertime, loans, adjustments, exemptions, or other compensation details. Use your official payslip as the final reference for your actual salary.
