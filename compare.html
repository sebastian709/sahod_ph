Salary planning guide

Why you keep less of a raise than you expect

Deductions don't grow evenly with your salary. SSS and Pag-IBIG stop at a ceiling, PhilHealth keeps rising until ₱100,000, and withholding tax takes a bigger share as you move up the brackets. Every extra peso of a raise is taxed at your top bracket, not your average rate.

Example: ₱42,000 to ₱50,000

Monthly ₱42,000 ₱50,000 Change
SSS₱1,750.00₱1,750.00₱0.00
PhilHealth₱1,050.00₱1,250.00+₱200.00
Pag-IBIG₱200.00₱200.00₱0.00
Withholding tax₱3,008.33₱4,568.33+₱1,560.00
Take-home pay₱35,991.67₱42,231.67+₱6,240.00

Of the ₱8,000 raise, SSS takes nothing because it's already at its ₱1,750 cap. PhilHealth takes 2.5% (₱200). The remaining ₱7,800 of taxable income sits in the 20% bracket, so tax takes ₱1,560. You keep ₱6,240, or 78% of the raise.

Asking for a take-home target

If you want a specific amount more in hand each month, divide it by the share you keep. At ₱42,000, you keep about 78% of each extra peso. So to take home ₱10,000 more a month, you would need roughly ₱10,000 ÷ 0.78 ≈ ₱12,800 more in gross salary. Enter the numbers above to check.

What else to compare besides monthly pay

  • 13th month pay grows with your basic salary. In the example above, the higher salary also adds ₱8,000 a year to your 13th month pay. It's tax-free up to ₱90,000, so the full annual gain is ₱74,880 + ₱8,000 = ₱82,880.
  • Non-taxable allowances are worth more per peso. De minimis benefits like a rice subsidy or laundry allowance, within BIR limits, aren't taxed. A ₱2,000 rice subsidy is worth ₱2,000 to you, while ₱2,000 more in basic pay is worth about ₱1,560 at the 20% bracket.
  • Allowances usually don't count toward 13th month pay, overtime, or holiday pay. An offer that shifts pay from basic salary into allowances lowers all of these.
  • HMO coverage, leave credits, and work arrangement. Commuting costs and time can easily be worth thousands of pesos a month.

Common questions

Salary comparison FAQs

Can a raise ever lower my take-home pay?

No. Philippine withholding tax is progressive, so only the part of your income above each bracket threshold is taxed at the higher rate. A raise always leaves you with more, just not the full amount.

Why does the take-home difference shrink at higher salaries?

Higher salaries fall into higher tax brackets: 20% from ₱33,333 of taxable income, 25% from ₱66,667, and 30% from ₱166,667. PhilHealth also keeps rising until ₱100,000.

Should I compare offers by gross or take-home pay?

Use take-home pay for your budget and gross pay for negotiating. Then add the benefits that aren't in either figure: 13th month pay, bonuses, allowances, and HMO.
