Salary planning guide

How to compare two salaries

Comparing job offers based only on the advertised monthly salary can be misleading. A higher gross salary does not always mean the same increase in take-home pay because applicable government contributions and withholding tax can affect the amount you receive.

1. Enter your current monthly salary

Enter the monthly salary you currently earn. SahodPH uses this amount to estimate your gross pay and take-home pay using the same calculation rules used by the salary calculator.

2. Enter the new monthly salary

Enter the salary offered by the new employer or the salary you want to compare. The calculator estimates the corresponding take-home pay using the same calculation method.

3. Review the salary difference

The comparison shows the difference between the two salaries before and after the estimated deductions. It also shows the percentage increase so you can quickly see how much larger the new gross salary is compared with the current salary.

4. Look at the annual difference

The monthly difference is also annualized to help you understand the potential yearly difference between the two salary amounts. This can be useful when evaluating a job offer or planning your expected income.

Example: comparing ₱42,000 and ₱50,000

Suppose your current monthly salary is ₱42,000 and a new job offers ₱50,000 per month. The gross difference is ₱8,000 per month, but the increase in take-home pay can be smaller after applicable deductions and withholding tax.

The calculator helps you compare both amounts using the same calculation rules, making it easier to see the estimated monthly and annual difference.

Gross salary increase vs. take-home increase

A salary increase should not be evaluated only by looking at the difference in gross pay. Your actual increase in take-home pay may be smaller because the additional income can affect applicable deductions and withholding tax.

This is especially useful when comparing two job offers with different salary amounts. Looking at estimated take-home pay gives you another number to consider alongside benefits, bonuses, allowances, work location, schedule, and other parts of the compensation package.

What this comparison does not include

The comparison is based on the salary information entered into the calculator. Your actual compensation may also include factors that are not represented in this comparison, such as:

  • Company loans or salary advances
  • Health insurance or HMO benefits
  • Bonuses and other incentives
  • Meal, transportation, or other allowances
  • Paid time off and leave benefits
  • Other employer-specific deductions or benefits

Disclaimer: The comparison is an estimate for informational and planning purposes. Actual payroll results may differ because of compensation structure, benefits, exemptions, payroll adjustments, employer policies, and applicable government rules.

Common questions

Salary comparison FAQs

Should I compare gross salary or take-home pay?

Both can be useful. Gross salary shows the difference between the offers before deductions, while estimated take-home pay gives you a better idea of how the salary difference may affect the amount you receive after the deductions included in the calculator.

Why is the take-home difference smaller than the gross difference?

Government contributions and withholding tax can reduce the amount of additional income that reaches your take-home pay. The effect depends on the salaries being compared and the assumptions used by the calculator.

Does the calculator include company benefits?

No. The comparison focuses on the salary and deductions modeled by SahodPH. Employer-specific benefits, loans, insurance, allowances, and other compensation may need to be evaluated separately.

Can I use this when comparing job offers?

Yes. Comparing estimated gross and take-home pay can be a useful part of evaluating two offers. You should also compare benefits, bonuses, allowances, commute, working hours, leave policies, and other employment conditions.

Is the result my exact future salary?

No. It is an estimate. Actual payroll can differ because of employer-specific deductions, compensation details, adjustments, exemptions, and applicable payroll rules.
