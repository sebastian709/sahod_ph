Holiday pay guide

How holiday pay works in the Philippines

Philippine holidays fall into two groups, and they are paid very differently.

  • Regular holidays are paid even if you don't work. If you do work, you get double pay. This comes from Article 94 of the Labor Code.
  • Special non-working days follow "no work, no pay". If you work, you get a 30% premium.
Holiday pay = basic daily rate × holiday multiplier

Worked examples

Using a basic daily rate of ₱1,931.03 (₱42,000 a month on a Monday-to-Friday schedule):

Scenario Computation Pay for the day
Worked on Independence Day (12 June)₱1,931.03 × 2.00₱3,862.06
Stayed home on Labor Day (1 May)₱1,931.03 × 1.00₱1,931.03

Worked on a special non-working day₱1,931.03 × 1.30₱2,510.34

Monthly-paid vs. daily-paid employees

Daily-paid employees see each holiday as a separate line on the payslip, so the table above is what gets added.

Monthly-paid employees are different. Their monthly salary already includes pay for unworked regular holidays, because the 261, 313, and 365-day factors all count regular holidays as paid days. So when you work a regular holiday, your payslip usually shows only the extra 100% as "holiday pay" or "holiday premium". In the example above, that's ₱1,931.03 on top of your normal salary. For a special day, the extra is 30% (₱579.31).

When you get paid for an unworked regular holiday

The implementing rules of the Labor Code say you are entitled to pay for an unworked regular holiday if you were present at work, or on leave with pay, on the workday immediately before the holiday. If you were absent without pay that day, you can lose the holiday pay unless you work on the holiday itself.

Special non-working days change every year. They are set by presidential proclamation and usually include days such as Ninoy Aquino Day (21 August), All Saints' Day (1 November), the Feast of the Immaculate Conception (8 December), and the last day of the year. Check the proclamation for the current year on the Official Gazette. Holiday dates can also be moved by proclamation.

A double holiday happens when two regular holidays fall on the same date. This has happened when Araw ng Kagitingan (9 April) fell on Maundy Thursday or Good Friday.

Who is not covered

Holiday pay under Article 94 does not apply to government employees, managerial employees, field personnel, and workers paid by results. It also doesn't apply to retail and service establishments that regularly employ fewer than ten workers.

Common questions

Holiday pay FAQs

Do I get paid on a regular holiday if I don't work?

Yes, 100% of your daily rate, as long as you were present or on paid leave on the workday before the holiday.

Do I get paid on a special non-working day if I don't work?

Not by law. The rule is "no work, no pay", unless your company policy, practice, or collective bargaining agreement says otherwise.

What if a regular holiday falls on a Sunday that is my rest day?

If you work, you get 260% of your daily rate for the first 8 hours. If you don't work, you still get 100%.

Is holiday pay taxable?

Yes, for most employees. It counts as taxable compensation. Minimum wage earners are exempt from income tax on their holiday pay.

How is overtime on a holiday computed?

Each hour beyond 8 gets an extra 30% on the holiday hourly rate. For a regular holiday, that's 260% of your basic hourly rate. Use the overtime pay calculator for the exact amount.
