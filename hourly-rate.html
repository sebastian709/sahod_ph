Hourly rate guide

How to compute your hourly rate

Article 83 of the Labor Code sets normal working hours at no more than 8 a day. Your basic hourly rate is your daily rate spread over those 8 hours:

Hourly rate = basic daily rate ÷ 8
or, from a monthly salary: monthly basic salary × 12 ÷ annual factor ÷ 8

The meal break doesn't count. Article 85 gives at least 60 minutes for meals, and that time isn't paid, so an 8-to-5 schedule with an hour's lunch is 8 working hours, not 9.

What the hourly rate is used for

  • Overtime: 125% of the hourly rate on an ordinary day, 169% on a rest day, 260% on a regular holiday.
  • Night differential: at least 10% of the hourly rate for each hour worked from 10 PM to 6 AM.
  • Tardiness and undertime: late minutes are commonly deducted at the hourly rate ÷ 60 per minute.

Compressed workweeks and shorter days

Under an approved compressed workweek, for example four 10-hour days or five 9.6-hour days, your pay for the week doesn't change but each day is longer. Enter your scheduled hours per day to see the hourly equivalent. Part-time employees on fewer than 8 hours should enter their actual daily hours.

Common questions

Hourly rate FAQs

Should I divide by 8 or 9 hours?

By 8. A 9-hour day at the office usually includes a one-hour unpaid meal break, which isn't working time.

What is the hourly rate for ₱20,000 a month?

₱114.94 on a Monday-to-Friday schedule (₱20,000 × 12 ÷ 261 ÷ 8), or ₱95.85 on a Monday-to-Saturday schedule.

Is the hourly rate before or after deductions?

Before. It's a gross figure. SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG, and withholding tax are computed on your monthly pay, not per hour.
